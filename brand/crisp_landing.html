Why it isn't a filter

Three things a sharpening slider can't do.

Rebuilds, not stretches

Trained on video pushed through real H.264, H.265, VP9 and AV1 encoders, so it undoes the artifacts web video actually has — not a synthetic imitation of them.

Never costs you playback

It watches its own frame budget. If your machine can't keep up it steps aside and passes the original through. It will never stutter your video to look better.

Stays off when it can't help

If a video is already about as sharp as your screen, Crisp doesn't run at all, rather than spending your battery for no visible gain.

Straight answers

What it won't do.

  • It doesn't invent detail. It reconstructs what compression blurred. It will not hallucinate a face out of a smudge, and it isn't trying to.
  • The gain shrinks as compression gets worse. Measured against plain upscaling, the advantage is largest on low-resolution video that is still reasonably clean, and smaller on video that has been crushed — there is less real detail left to recover.
  • It needs WebGPU. A recent Chrome and a GPU. Without them Crisp simply stays off.
  • It can't touch DRM video. Netflix, Disney+ and the like are off limits to every extension, this one included.
